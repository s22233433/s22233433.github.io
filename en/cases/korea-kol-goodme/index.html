CASE STUDY / CROSS-MARKET STORY

Korean KOL x Goodme Tea

A Korean creator store visit that connected travel context, drink experience, and social storytelling into a clear brand narrative.

Market entry
Helping Korean audiences understand a Chinese tea store
Content approach
Store visit, drink choice, and cross-language context
Delivery role
Creator visit, published content, and link organization

DECISIONS / EXECUTION

Content decisions | Handover the context before the product

01

What should be localized first?

Explain the travel or city context before describing the drink so an unfamiliar brand has a clear reason to visit.

02

How can overseas content be more than a check-in?

Arrange the store cue, reason to visit, personal response, and brand information as checkable sections while keeping the creator’s voice.

03

What should acceptance examine?

Verify the published content and links first, then check language, commercial disclosure, and rights separately.

Project summary

Key judgment

For a beverage brand entering an unfamiliar market, the store, drink choice, and personal response need to form a credible visit story. A creator is not only there to show the product.

Background and objective

Goodme Tea wanted Korean audiences to understand the store and product experience of a Chinese tea brand, not simply see a drink.

Brand Objective

Use a tangible store visit to make an initial connection between the brand, city context, and everyday drinking occasion.

The unfamiliar-market challenge

When food content only describes a menu item, an unfamiliar audience has little reason to visit, little sense of the store, and no connection to a travel or everyday routine.

Creator screening dimensions

  • Travel, city exploration, and food-and-drink content fit naturally together.
  • Can turn a visit and personal response into a coherent story instead of reciting selling points.
  • Can realistically coordinate the store visit, filming timing, and on-site communication.
  • Recent content, collaboration reliability, and brand-safety fit can be reviewed in advance.

Store-visit content cues

01

Reason to visit

Start with a credible reason for the stop within travel, city exploration, or a daily route.

02

Drink choice and store experience

Use the selection process, space, and service experience to give the product context.

03

Personal response

Keep the creator's own tone and opinion instead of turning the content into a hard sell.

Execution checkpoints

01

Store and menu

Confirm visit timing, store details, and drinks that can be introduced accurately.

02

Brief and filming

Align required information, filming scope, non-misleading language, and on-site arrangements.

03

Review and disclosure

Confirm key messages, commercial disclosure, and the revision window.

04

Publish and handover

Record live links, rights status, and reusable delivery records.

Deliverables and reusable learning

The close-out is grounded in the published content and link record. The reusable lesson is not a fixed script: align the creator's reason to visit, story rhythm, and brand information in one workable brief.
