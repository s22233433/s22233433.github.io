Choosing Topics Through TikTok’s 2026 Trends: Turn Popular Searches into Questions You Can Answer

Starting with TikTok Next 2026’s search-and-discovery observations, use a commuter bag as an example to turn vague popular topics into specific questions, evidence shots, and a reusable topic-planning sheet.

ZhenguoCool Editorial · 2026.09.06

In this storyFirst understand what a trend report can sayTurn commuter-bag needs into questionsA small worksheet you can use without tools

Popular searches can show what words people are looking for, but they cannot directly answer “why someone is searching” or “whether they believe the answer.” They certainly cannot replace needs interviews or sales data. The first step in choosing a topic is to step back from the heat and ask a question you can genuinely answer on camera.

The TikTok Next trend forecast published on 2026-01-14 describes Curiosity Detours as a behavioral direction toward more active exploration and taking a detour to discover answers. It is TikTok’s forecast about platform culture, not an independent study of users in Taiwan; this article does not cite survey figures from the report or treat it as a proven need among Taiwanese audiences.

01 / First understand what a trend report can say

A trend report is useful for proposing a hypothesis that viewers may want to see how a question gets solved, not for proving that a topic will go viral. On 2024-03-13, TikTok introduced Creator Search Insights and explained that users in some regions can see search topics, categories, For You, and content gap; the entry point can be found by typing its name in the app’s search bar, while actual availability depends on the app and TikTok’s official guidance.

Do not conflate these terms

“Lots of searches” is a visible behavioral signal on the platform; “there is a need” requires more context; “will buy” is another proposition this exercise has not established.

02 / Turn commuter-bag needs into questions

The fictional “commuter bag” below is only an exercise: it does not pull any real posts or fill in platform data. Each question should be answerable in one limited shot, with the scope of the title stated clearly; if the only possible descriptions are “super useful” or “must buy,” there is not yet verifiable evidence.

When filming, do not casually copy someone else’s wording or shots. Write your own question first, then decide which visible condition to show; if the camera cannot answer it, narrow the title or mark it for research instead of using popularity to fill an evidence gap.

03 / A small worksheet you can use without tools

Creator Search Insights does not guarantee that every Taiwan account can see it; actual availability depends on the app and TikTok’s official guidance. The Help page can be a reference for further reading, but this article does not treat account-by-account eligibility that has not been checked as a universal rule. If you cannot find the entry point, do not work around the restriction or treat search-bar results as complete market data; work through one round using your own question worksheet first.

After publishing, do not look only at whether the topic drew views. Review whether comments pursue the same question, challenge a particular condition, or respond only to the editing style; put the new question into the next worksheet. This way, search signals are only a starting point—visual evidence and readers’ follow-up questions are what make topic selection more concrete over time.
