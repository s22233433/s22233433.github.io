Assess your partnership model

Brand-managed outreach

  • Fits teams with a creator database, knowledge of terms, and capacity to follow up.
  • It offers direct communication, but the work of chasing multiple creators, replacing them, managing rights, and reporting is often underestimated.
  • At minimum, define a brief, quote basis, review process, and live-content record first.

Platforms and talent agencies

  • Platforms help find creators quickly when the brand can validate content and run follow-up itself.
  • Talent agencies fit a known roster but may not cover cross-roster comparison or full campaign coordination.
  • Both can be useful sources; neither automatically completes brand-side project management.

Campaign agencies and project teams

  • Fit brands that need screening across creators, aligned terms, seeding, review, publishing, and close-out records.
  • Ask how rejected pitches, delays, revisions, and replacements are tracked, not just how many names are available.
  • Confirm what the service fee covers; do not mix creator fees, agency fees, and rights.

Zhenguo's boundary

  • Zhenguo focuses on creator campaign management, including selection rationale, execution records, and delivery organization.
  • It fits brands with clear product and campaign goals and a review owner.
  • It does not fit group buying, resale, or requests for guaranteed conversion outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

How is a platform different from an influencer marketing agency?

A platform mainly helps discovery and contact. A campaign partner connects screening, terms, content, timing, rights, and reporting into a trackable delivery.

What should I ask a potential partner?

Ask how creators are verified, how delays or drop-offs are handled, how rights are confirmed, and what records are delivered at close-out.

Is a lower service fee always better?

Not necessarily. Compare review, seeding, revisions, replacement, rights, and close-out scope first; a low fee may simply move the work back to the brand.
